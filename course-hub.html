What You Will Build

The Pipeline

Every assignment is one stage. By the end of the semester you own all of them.

Stages, in order

  1. Generate traffic and logs, including attacks you control.
  2. Collect them: agents, shipping, storage.
  3. Structure raw text into fields you can query.
  4. Preprocess: encode, scale, and deal with class imbalance.
  5. Train a detector, starting from a baseline you can beat.
  6. Infer on data the model has never seen.
  7. Alert, with triage and a false-positive budget.
  8. Dashboard: put the decision in front of a human.

Tools and data

  • Text first: awk, sed, regular expressions. A log line is text before it is anything else.
  • Datasets: NSL-KDD first, because it is small and well understood. It is not time-series, so we move to a second dataset with real ordering before the reproduction work begins.
  • Column reference: every NSL-KDD column, verified against the files. Ranges recomputed from the data, with the five places the circulating feature table is wrong marked in amber.
  • Models: classical baselines, LSTMs, transformers, parameter-efficient tuning of language models, and reinforcement learning for response.
  • Everything in Python, in a repository, reproducible by someone who is not you.
If you cannot rerun it from a clean checkout, it does not count as working.

The Main Event

Reproduce a Paper

Your capstone is a reproduction study: take a recent intrusion-detection paper and rebuild it end to end.

How to choose a paper

  • Published in the last three years, in a venue with a high h5-index in security and cryptography.
  • Check the Google Scholar venue rankings for computer security and cryptography before you commit.
  • It must describe a pipeline you can actually run: data you can obtain, methods described in enough detail to rebuild.
  • Get it approved before you start. Two teams may not reproduce the same paper.

What you hand in

  • A repository that runs from a clean checkout.
  • A Wiki report: what the paper claimed, what you got, and where the two diverge.
  • A presentation to the class.
A failed reproduction is a valid result. Report the discrepancy honestly, with evidence. That is how the field works, and it is graded as a success.

The Fine Print, Digested

Policies You'll Actually Ask About

The full versions live in the course outline and syllabus on Canvas - this is the quick reference.

Late work

  • No late work is accepted. Every deliverable, assignments and capstone components alike, is due at the stated time. After that the grade for that item is zero.
  • This includes the capstone final deliverables: the Wiki reproduction report, the repository, the project board and the presentation.
  • Documented emergency? Contact Dr. Parra before the deadline. Accommodations are arranged in advance, not afterwards.

Attendance

  • Attendance is mandatory and worth 10%. Up to 3 absences without penalty, excused or not. The 4th absence forfeits the entire 10%.
  • Arriving 10+ minutes late counts as tardy; 3 tardies = 1 unexcused absence.
  • Planned university/religious/military absences: written notice at least two weeks ahead. They still count toward the three.

Academic integrity

Highest standards of academic honesty apply. Cheating and plagiarism are handled per the UIW Student Handbook. Using AI tools is expected in this course; passing off work you cannot explain is not.
