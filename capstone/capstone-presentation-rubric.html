Capstone Presentation Rubric

CIS 3370 Intrusion Detection Systems  |  Instructor: Dr. Gonzalo D Parra
The final presentation is worth 10% of your course grade (part of the 45% capstone). Teams present in posted time slots; the order is posted on Canvas. Each team gets 10 to 15 minutes plus Q&A. Every member presents the user stories they contributed to.

Hard Rules

  • Every member presents. Delivery credit requires each member to present the user stories they contributed to.
  • Missing your slot costs 15%. A missed scheduled presentation slot is an automatic 15% deduction from the team's total capstone grade.
  • No late final work. The final wiki, repo, board, and presentation are not accepted late.
  • Demo fallback required. A recorded demo must be in the repo. If the live demo fails, switch to the recording and keep going; a working artifact is not penalized heavily for a failed cable.

Required Structure (Build, Attack, Defend)

  1. The claim you set out to reproduce, and the result you measured, side by side.
  2. Which course modules you integrated (at least three) and how they connect.
  3. The attack: demonstrate the threat, ideally showing it blocked live after defenses.
  4. The defenses: which controls you implemented and how you verified them, with before/after.
  5. Residual risk and lessons learned, honestly stated.

Criteria Preview (matches the grading sheet)

These are the same criteria and weights the instructor uses on the grading rubric. The presentation is scored out of 100 and converts to the 10% component.

Technical demo

40%
ExcellentA live demo shows the attack being blocked or a policy being enforced, not just described. The before/after is convincing and the team drives it confidently.
AdequateA demo runs (live or via the required recording) and shows the defense working, but part of it is described rather than shown.
Missing or weakNo working demo shown live or recorded, or the demo does not actually show a control blocking the attack or enforcing a policy.

Content and architecture

30%
ExcellentA clear account of what was built, which course modules were integrated, and an insightful walkthrough of the architecture that a listener could follow and redraw.
AdequateWhat was built and which modules were used are covered, but the architecture walkthrough is thin or the module connections are vague.
Missing or weakThe build is unclear, fewer than three modules are shown, or the architecture is not explained.

Professionalism and Q&A

30%
ExcellentPracticed and in time (10 to 15 minutes), every member speaks to their user stories, and the team shows mastery answering questions.
AdequateEvery member speaks but parts are uneven, timing slips, or some answers wander.
Missing or weakA member does not present, the talk runs well over or under time, or questions go unanswered.
Missed slot deduction: failing to present in your scheduled slot is an automatic 15% deduction from the team's total capstone grade.

Tips

  • Lead with the live demo. Open by showing the attack blocked or the policy enforced; it earns the most and sets the tone.
  • Show before/after of the attack. Demonstrate the threat succeeding against the undefended environment, then show it stopped after your control.
  • Be honest about residual risk. Saying "this still partially works, here is our compensating control" reads as maturity, not weakness.
  • Rehearse handoffs and timing. Practice the full run so every member's part is smooth and you land inside 10 to 15 minutes.
  • Have the recording ready. If the live demo fails, switch to the recorded fallback and keep going.
