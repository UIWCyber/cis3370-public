6

Two Questions People Confuse

  1. 1Precision: of the alarms you raised, how many were real? It is about wasted attention.
  2. 2Recall: of the attacks that happened, how many did you catch? It is about missed intrusions.
  3. 3They move in opposite directions. Raising one lowers the other, always, and the choice is yours to make.
AnalogyA smoke alarm with perfect recall goes off when you make toast. A smoke alarm with perfect precision is one you disabled, and it is silent during the fire.

Why It Misses Exactly Those

  1. 1DoS and Probe are loud: many connections at once, odd flags, obvious byte counts. They are easy to describe with a rule.
  2. 2R2L and U2R look like normal use, because they are normal use by the wrong person.
  3. 3The training file has 995 R2L rows and 52 U2R rows to learn from. The test file has 2,885 and 67.
Why it mattersThis is Lecture 10's imbalance arriving as a consequence. A model does not fail evenly. It fails where the examples were thin and the signal was subtle, which is where the serious attacks live.

Read the Curve, and Its Limit

DefinitionArea under the ROC curve. The chance that the model gives a random attack a higher score than a random normal connection. 0.5 is a coin, 1.0 is perfect ranking.
  1. 1Naive Bayes 0.939, logistic regression 0.853, the tree 0.834. By this number Naive Bayes is clearly the best model.
  2. 2The area ignores the threshold entirely, which is its use and its limit: it says the model ranks well, not that any line you can afford works.
  3. 3And Naive Bayes, the best ranker here, catches zero U2R rows. A number can be correct and still point at the wrong model.

Read the Collapse

  3. 3Always: state the prevalence. A metric without the base rate is not a measurement.

From a Rate to a Day's Work

  • A sensor that sees 100,000 connections a day, of which 99 percent are normal. That is an assumption, and it is stated on the slide because it is not measured from our files.
  1. 199,000 normal connections a day meet the model.
  2. 2The logistic regression's false alarm rate at threshold 0.5 is 0.074.
  3. 30.074 times 99,000 is 7,309 alarms a day, every day, all of them wrong.
DefinitionFalse-positive budget. The number of false alarms a team can actually look at in a day, decided before the model is chosen. A team of two analysts can triage roughly 50.

Read the Distance

  1. 1At the default 0.5 the sensor raises 7,309 alarms a day against a budget of 50. That is 146 times over.
  2. 2At 0.95, the strictest threshold in the sweep, it still raises 6,239 a day and catches fewer attacks.
  3. 3No threshold in the sweep comes inside the budget. Not one. The model is not deployable on that sensor as it stands.
Why it mattersA false alarm rate of 7 percent looks small in a report and is fatal in an operations centre, because it is multiplied by every normal connection, and normal is almost everything.

Decide: What You Do About It

  1. 1Say it. The honest report is "at any threshold we tested this model exceeds the alarm budget by two orders of magnitude".
  2. 2Change the question. Detect per host or per session rather than per connection, so there are fewer things to be wrong about.
  3. 3Change the target. A model aimed at one attack family, with its own threshold, can be affordable when a general one is not.
  4. 4Do not quietly raise the threshold until the alarm count looks nice and report the accuracy from before.
TipAsk for the alarm budget in the first meeting, not the last. It decides which models are even candidates, and it costs nothing to ask.

Out of the Bag

  • Each tree in a forest is fitted on a resample, so about a third of the rows are left out of each tree. Scoring every row on the trees that did not see it is free.
CIS 3370 Intrusion Detection Systems
31

Read the Gap, Which You Have Seen Before

  1. 1Out-of-bag error says the forest gets 1 row in 1,000 wrong. The test file says it gets 229 in 1,000 wrong.
  2. 2Both are held-out scores. The difference is where the rows were held out from: out-of-bag rows come from the training file, and the training file has no U2R attack types it never saw.
  3. 3This is the same gap as Lecture 11's cross-validation, reached by a different road. Held-out training rows measure stability. Only new data measures novelty.
Watch outAny score computed from the training file, however cleverly held out, shares the training file's blind spots. That includes cross-validation, out-of-bag error, and every number a library hands you for free.

Decide: What To Report, and In What Order

  1. 1The four counts, so a reader can compute anything else themselves.
  2. 2Recall per family, with counts, because the average hides the rare classes.
  3. 3The threshold, why that one, and the alarms a day it implies on the sensor you actually have.
  4. 4The prevalence beside any curve number.
  5. 5Accuracy last, if at all, with the floor beside it.
Watch outA report that leads with accuracy on this dataset is either uninformed or hoping you are. You now know enough to notice, which means you also have to write your own reports differently.
