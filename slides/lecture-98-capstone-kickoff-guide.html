Why Reproduction and Not Invention

  • Building something new that nobody can check is easy. This is harder and worth more.
  • Reproduction is how the field decides what is real.
  • You will read a paper the way an engineer reads a spec, not the way a student reads a summary.
Why it mattersEmployers do not ask whether you have heard of transformers. They ask whether you can make a published method run on their data.

A Failed Reproduction Is a Result

  • What is graded is the rigour of the attempt and the honesty of the report.

Choosing a Paper: The Reproducibility Test

  • Data: can you actually get it? Public dataset, or one you can regenerate.
  • Method: described in enough detail to rebuild, not just cited.
  • Metrics: stated precisely enough to recompute.
  • Scale: trainable on hardware you have, in the time you have.
CIS 3370 Intrusion Detection Systems

Papers That Will Sink You

  • Private or licensed data you cannot obtain.
  • A method described only as "we used a deep neural network".
  • Results reported without the metric definition or the split.
  • Anything needing a compute budget you do not have.
  • A paper another team already claimed. First approved, first served.

The Proposal

  1. 1Name the paper, the venue, and the year.
  2. 2State the exact claim you will reproduce, as a number or a table.
  3. 3Show that you can already load the data.
  4. 4Name the risk most likely to stop you, and your fallback.
  • The proposal is worth 2% and must be approved before you start building.

Teams

  • Every member needs a real share of the pipeline, not a job title.
Watch outA team that divides work as "one does code, one does slides" always produces a weak reproduction and an unfair grade split.

Compare Schedules Before You Commit

  1. 1Each member lists the hours they can actually work in a week.
  2. 2Overlay them and find your shared blocks.
  3. 3If you have fewer than three shared hours a week, you are not a team yet.
  4. 4Agree the weekly meeting time now and put it in the repository.

Vote on Your Commit Approach

  • Every team votes, unanimously, and declares the choice in the proposal.
  • Individual Commits: each member pushes their own work under their own account.
  • Delegated Commits: one member pushes on behalf of others, naming them in the message.
NoteIndividual Commits makes contribution visible without anyone having to argue about it later. Choose Delegated only for a stated reason.

What Goes in the Wiki Report

  • The claim you set out to reproduce, quoted precisely.
  • What you did, in enough detail that a reader could repeat it.
  • Your numbers next to theirs, in a table.
  • Where the two diverge, and your best explanation of why.
  • What you would need to close the gap.

Evidence Standards

  • A result with no command that produced it is an opinion.
  • Every number in the report traces to something in the repository.
  • Random seeds, splits and versions are part of the result.
Why it mattersThe whole point of the project is that someone else can check you. Hold yourself to what you are holding the paper's authors to.

Reading the Weights

  • The reproduction itself is the largest single piece, at 15%.
  • Reporting and presenting together are worth more than the build, at 20%.
  • The repository and its board carry 8%, because how the team worked is evidence too.
  • That ratio is deliberate. Work nobody can follow does not count in this field.

Progress Presentations

How Teams Lose Marks

  • Leaving the reproduction until after the progress presentations.
  • One member holding all the code, so nobody else can run it.
  • Numbers in the report that no script produces.
  • Discovering in week 12 that the data was never obtainable.
TipEvery one of these is visible in the repository weeks before it becomes a grade. So is the fix.
