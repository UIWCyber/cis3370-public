Two Different Reasons a Model Fails on Test

  1. 1Overfitting: it learned the training rows themselves, so anything new confuses it.
  2. 2Novelty: the test set contains attack types that were never in training, so no amount of learning helps.
  3. 3NSL-KDD has both, on purpose, which is why every number here drops.
DefinitionOverfitting. Performance that is much better on data the model has seen than on data it has not, because the model learned the rows rather than the pattern.

The Memoriser Won, Which Is Awkward

  • The unlimited tree scored a perfect 1.000 on training. Textbook overfitting.
  • It also scored the best test result of the four, at 0.805.
  1. 1A perfect training score is a warning, not a verdict.
  2. 2The depth-5 tree is 1.1 points worse on test and much easier to read.
  3. 3If two models are within a point of each other, take the one you can explain to an analyst at 3 AM.
Watch outDo not teach yourself the rule "training accuracy 1.0 means the model is broken". Teach yourself to look at the gap, then at what the model does with the classes you care about.

Cross-Validation, in Plain Words

  • Split the training data five ways. Train five times, each time holding out a different fifth. You get five scores instead of one.
  1. 1The average tells you the expected performance.
  2. 2The spread tells you how much the answer depends on which rows you happened to train on.
  3. 3A wide spread on a large dataset means the model is unstable, and a single score was hiding it.
TipReport the spread, not just the mean. "0.79, give or take 0.01" is a claim. "0.79" is a hope.

What the Weights Say, and What They Do Not

  • Logistic regression gives one weight per column, so it looks explainable. Read the weights carefully.
  1. 1A large weight on a one-hot column means that one service or flag value moves the answer.
  2. 2Weights are only comparable because you standardized every column last session.
  3. 3A weight is a correlation the model found, not a cause. service_private being predictive does not make it guilty.
AnalogyThe weights are the model's notes about which columns it found useful. Notes are evidence about the note-taker as much as about the world.

Choosing Between Four Models

  1. 1Need to explain a decision to a human? The shallow tree, or the regression.
  2. 2Need the best test number and can accept a black box? The unlimited tree.
  3. 3Need to answer in microseconds on every packet? Not k-NN.
  4. 4Need to detect the rare families? None of these yet, which is the next session.
NoteYou have four models that all beat the floor and all miss most of what matters. That is not a failure of the session, it is the setup for the evaluation session, where accuracy stops being allowed as the only number.
