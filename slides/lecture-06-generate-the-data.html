A Baseline Is Normal, Measured

  • An everyday version. You know your electricity bill is wrong because you know what it usually is. Nobody told you the right number. You have months of old bills.
  • Anomaly detection works the same way. It compares now against normal, and someone has to have written normal down, with numbers.
DefinitionBaseline. A set of measurements taken while the system is behaving as usual, kept so later measurements can be compared against it.
Why it mattersWithout a baseline, every alert is an opinion. With one, an alert is a comparison, and a comparison can be argued with.

Decide: the Band Is a Description, Not a Detector

  1. 1A rule that fires once in five minutes of ordinary traffic is not catching anything. It is describing the traffic.
  2. 2The threshold, written down: more than 87 connections in one second is worth looking at. You now also know it will almost never fire, which is the useful half of knowing it.
Why it mattersThis is the first time in the course you measure normal and find normal too wide to be useful. It is not a broken measurement. It is what quiet traffic looks like, and it is why the Alert session spends a whole day on thresholds.

Never Hardcode the Interface Name

  • eth0 on one machine is en0 on another and Ethernet 2 on a third. A script that names one is a script that runs on one machine.
Ask the routing table which interface actually carries traffic
$ ip route | grep default | awk '{print $5}'# Linux
$ route get default | awk '/interface:/ {print $2}'# macOS
TipThe rule generalises past interfaces: anything that differs between machines gets discovered at run time, never typed into the script.

A Counter Counts Up Forever

  • rx_bytes is not how much traffic there is. It is how much there has been since boot.
DefinitionCumulative counter. A number that only increases. The quantity you want is the difference between two readings, divided by the time between them.
Watch outPlot a cumulative counter and you get a line going up, on every machine, always. It looks like a working chart and it says nothing.

Decide: Never Plot the Counter

Why it mattersBoth panels are the same numbers. The left one answers "how much has there been", which nobody asked. The right one answers "is something happening now", which is the only question a detector asks.
  1. 1The rule, written down: if a column only ever goes up, it is a counter. Take the difference between consecutive rows, divide by the seconds between them, and plot that.
  2. 2ports and connections are not counters. They count what is true right now, so plot them as they are.

A Log Is a Summary, a Capture Is the Evidence

  • A log line is something a program decided to write down. A packet capture is what actually crossed the wire.
  • Captures are large, they hold everything, and everything includes things you did not mean to record.
AnalogyA log is the receipt. A capture is the security camera footage of the whole shop. One is easier to file; the other settles arguments.
Watch outCapture only on a machine you own, on your own connection. Never on the university network, never on a shared or public network, and never on someone else's device.

Rules of Engagement, Before You Attack Anything

  • Scan your own machine, from your own machine. Nothing else, on any network, at any time.
  • Not the university network. Not a roommate's laptop. Not a device you administer at work.
  • A scan is noisy and it is logged. On a network you do not own, it is a security incident with your name on it.
  • If you are unsure whether something is in scope, it is not.

Decide: Report the Ratio, Do Not Ship It

Spike ruleSeconds it fires on, out of 299
3 times the baseline median119

  1. 1A ratio to the median is the wrong shape of threshold for this traffic. An analyst would see the 3-times rule fire 119 times in five minutes.
  2. 2Report the ratio for the scan you ran, because you know when it happened and the ratio says how loud it was.
  3. 3Do not turn it into a rule. Your scan will give a big ratio and you will want to. This table is the measurement that says do not, and the Alert session picks a threshold that can be shipped.

What a Detector Would Have Needed

  • You found the scan because you knew when you ran it. A detector has no such advantage.
  1. 1It needs the same measurement taken continuously, not once when you remembered.
  2. 2It needs a quiet baseline to compare against, which is the file you just made.
  3. 3It needs the packets turned into records with names, which is Stage 2 and Stage 3.
  4. 4It needs a threshold someone chose on purpose, which is the Alert session.
NoteNext session replaces your two scripts with Zeek and Suricata, which do this continuously and write records with field names. Everything you measured by hand today, they measure by default.
