A Dashboard Is Not a Summary

  • It is a tool for a person who has to decide something, under time pressure, repeatedly.
  • If nobody can name the decision, you are building decoration.

Three Questions Before Any Panel

  1. 1Who looks at this, and how often? An analyst every ten minutes reads differently from a director once a month.
  2. 2What decision does it support? Escalate or not, investigate or not, call someone or not.
  3. 3What would change their answer? That is the number the panel has to show.
Watch outA panel nobody can act on still costs a query on every refresh, and it trains people to ignore the screen. Removing panels is as much of the work as adding them.

Saved Objects Are the Reproducible Part

  1. 1A dashboard is data: panels, queries, references to data views, all as JSON.
  2. 2Exporting it means someone else can rebuild your screen exactly, which is the same argument as committing your code.
  3. 3It also means a dashboard can be reviewed in a pull request, like anything else you would defend.
DefinitionSaved object. Kibana's stored definition of a dashboard, visualization or data view, exportable as newline-delimited JSON and importable anywhere.
Watch outKibana imports saved objects from its own major version or the one before it, and no further. The dashboards from a stack two majors back cannot be imported at all, which is why this course builds its own.

The Four Choices in Every Panel

  1. 1Data view: which index the panel reads.
  2. 2Vertical axis: what is counted or measured.
  3. 3Horizontal axis: what it is broken down along, usually time or a category.
  4. 4Breakdown: an optional second split, drawn as colour.
TipBuild with a bar chart first even when you want something else. If the bars say nothing, no other chart type will save the panel.

Arrange It the Way a Person Reads

  1. 1Top left is read first, so put the number that decides "is anything wrong" there.
  2. 2Detail goes below or to the right, for the person who has already decided to look.
  3. 3Anything that needs a paragraph of explanation is a report, not a panel.

Time Range Is Part of the Panel

  • The same dashboard says different things at fifteen minutes, one day and thirty days.
  1. 1An analyst wants the last hour and cares about spikes.
  2. 2A manager wants the last month and cares about the trend.
  3. 3Save the dashboard with a default range that matches its audience, and say so in the description.
Watch outEvery panel you build today will look empty tomorrow if you leave the default range on the last fifteen minutes and your data is a fixed capture. That is the same trap from the Store session, and it will catch someone again.

What a Dashboard Cannot Do

  1. 1It cannot tell you why. Every panel is a count, and counts do not carry causes.
  2. 2It cannot prove a negative. An empty panel means nothing matched the query you wrote.
  3. 3It cannot replace the report. The four sentences you have written all term are the deliverable; the dashboard is the instrument.
Why it mattersThe most common failure in a security operations centre is a beautiful dashboard nobody can act on, watched by people who have stopped expecting it to tell them anything.

The Loop, on Your Dashboard

  1. 1Analysis: which panels you built and the numbers they show.
  2. 2Issue: the panel that cannot support a decision, or the range that hides the thing you care about.
  3. 3Tools: Lens, the data view, the saved object export.
  4. 4Solution: the panel you would delete, and the one you would add given another week of data.
