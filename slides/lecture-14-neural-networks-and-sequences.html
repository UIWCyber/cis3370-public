Which Switch, and When

  1. 1ReLU in hidden layers, because it is cheap and it does not flatten out for large inputs.

Walking Downhill in Fog

  • An everyday version. You are on a hillside in thick fog and want the bottom. You cannot see it, but you can feel which way is down under your feet. Take a step that way. Feel again. Repeat.

The Step Size Has a Name

DefinitionLearning rate. The size of the step gradient descent takes. It is the one setting that will waste your afternoon if it is wrong, and the loss curve tells you which way to move it.
  • Every framework calls it this, and every framework gives you a default that is usually close.

17

Read the Trade, and Decide

  1. 1Reading all 20,000 rows at every step costs 1,200,000 row readings over 60 steps, and ends at loss 0.3219.
  2. 2Reading 8 rows at every step costs 480 row readings, and ends at 0.3302.
  3. 3The small batch is noisier because 8 rows are a rough guess at which way is downhill. On average the guess points the right way, which is enough.
DefinitionMini-batch. Using a small random handful of rows for each step instead of the whole file. Every practical network is trained this way, because the file does not fit in memory and the noise costs almost nothing.

The Two Things That Table Says

  1. 1The network is worth it. 32 neurons beat the depth-5 tree by 2.3 points and caught 3.7 points more attacks, for 3.4 seconds of training. That is a real gain and it is the first one in this course.
  2. 2Bigger is not better. 128 neurons is four times the weights of 32 and scores lower. A second layer scores lower. All of them memorised the training file at 0.997 or above.
  3. 3The extra capacity went into fitting training rows the model had already fitted, which is not what you were paid for.
Watch out"Add a layer" is the most common wrong answer in this field. Measure it. If it does not help, say so and keep the smaller model, which is also the faster one to run on every connection forever.

Reading a Sentence One Word at a Time

  • An everyday version. "The bank was closed" means one thing after "I went to the river" and another after "I went to deposit a cheque". The words before change what the current word means.
DefinitionSequence model. It reads rows in order and carries a small summary of what it has seen so far, updating that summary at every row. An LSTM is one that can choose what to keep, update or forget.

The Cheap Version: a Window

  1. 1Instead of a memory, hand the model the last few rows as extra columns. Eight rows of 107 columns becomes one example of 856 columns.
  2. 2The label is the label of the last row in the window.
  3. 3It is cruder than a real memory and it costs nothing to try, so try it first.
Watch outBuild windows first and split second, and rows from the same window land on both sides of your split. Split first, always.

27

Read the Null Result, and Why It Was Predictable

  1. 1Windows are worse: 0.754 against 0.798, and 0.634 of attacks caught against 0.677.
  2. 2Lecture 4 measured why. The file order of KDDTrain+ carries no dependence at all: the next row has the same label 50.3 percent of the time, and 50.2 percent is what independence would give.
  3. 3So the seven extra rows in each window are seven rows of noise, and the model has eight times as many weights to fit with the same information.
Why it mattersThis is the honest answer for this file, and it is the reason the sequence work belongs on a dataset with real timestamps. On ordered data the same experiment comes out the other way, which is exactly why you run the experiment rather than assuming.

Decide: When a Sequence Model Is Worth It

  1. 1Measure the order first. Lecture 4's test takes one line: how often does the next row share the label, and what would independence give? If those two numbers match, stop.
  2. 2Try the window before the memory. It is a few lines and it answers the question cheaply.
  3. 3Bring your hand-built features anyway. A sequence model with good features beats one that has to discover them, and you already know how to build them.

What To Report About a Network

  1. 1The architecture in one line: layers, sizes, activation.
  2. 2The number of weights, because it is the honest measure of what you asked the data to support.
  3. 3How many passes, and whether the loss was still falling when you stopped.
  4. 4The comparison with your simplest model, always.
Why it mattersA network reported without its baseline is unreadable. Two points better than a tree is a result. 0.817 on its own is a number.
