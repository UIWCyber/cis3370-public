Eight Lines Before Any Data

The top of conn.log

DefinitionSelf-describing file. One that carries its own field names and types, so a reader does not need a separate document to parse it correctly.

When the Summary and the Detail Disagree

  • conn.log says 3,258 connections were ssl. But ssl.log describes 3,261 connections.
  1. 1Three connections have a full TLS record and no service name in the summary.
  2. 2In http.log the same thing happens 42 times.
  3. 3Neither file is lying. The summary field is a conclusion, and the conclusion was not reached.
TipTwo files that disagree by a small number is where findings live. Chase the three, not the 3,258.

An Alert Is a Rule That Fired

  • It is a claim, made by a signature someone wrote, about traffic it matched.
  • It is evidence. It is not a verdict, and it is not a fact about intent.
Watch outThe word "alert" makes people act. Before acting, read the rule name: it usually says exactly what it matched, and often that is less than the name suggests.

So You Join on What They Both Saw

  • Source address, destination address, destination port, and a time window. That is all you have.
  1. 1544 distinct alerting flows in the bundle.
  2. 2449 of them, which is 83 percent, match a connection in conn.log.
  3. 3The other 17 percent began before the five-minute window opened.
Why it mattersThe 83 percent is not a bug in the tools, it is the cost of two tools that never agreed on a name. Stage 3 is where we make them agree.

What a Shipper Must Not Do

  • Lose records. Everything downstream inherits the gap and nothing reports it.
  1. 1Your Stage 1 baseline had rx_drop for this reason: a collector that drops is measuring itself.
  2. 2A queue that fills silently is the same failure one layer up.
  3. 3The honest question about any shipper is what it does when the far end is unreachable.
